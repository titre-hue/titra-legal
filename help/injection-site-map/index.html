Using titra

How the injection-site map works in titra

The map is a record of where each injection went. It shows this dose's site, your last two and a suggested next one, and it zooms in so you can mark the exact spot.

The titra team·Last checked 16 November 2026·4 min read·What changed

The injection-site map is a record of where each dose went. On a body outline it shows the site for this dose, the last two you used and a suggested next one. From there you can zoom into one area and mark the exact spot on a grid.

Sources checked 12 November 2026

  • 1Semaglutide injection, weekly pen: prescribing information and instructions for use (DailyMed, US National Library of Medicine)
  • 2Tirzepatide injection: prescribing information and instructions for use (DailyMed, US National Library of Medicine)

It is part of the dose log for injected medicines. For tablets the log has no site step. The map records and reminds, and that is all it does: where to inject is set out in the instructions that come with your pen and by whoever prescribed it.

Sources checked 12 November 2026 (2)
  • 1Semaglutide injection, weekly pen: prescribing information and instructions for use (DailyMed, US National Library of Medicine)
  • 2Tirzepatide injection: prescribing information and instructions for use (DailyMed, US National Library of Medicine)

The ten sites

The outline has ten named sites:

  • four on the abdomen: upper left, upper right, lower left and lower right
  • two on each thigh: outer and front
  • one on each upper arm

Those are the three areas named in the instructions for use of the injected medicines titra lists: the stomach area, the thigh and the back of the upper arm [1][2]. Left and right are as you look down at yourself.

What the marks mean

Mark Meaning
Filled coloured dot The site chosen for this dose
Dark dot with a 1 The site you used last time
Grey dot with a 2 The site you used the time before that
Dashed green ring The suggested site
Plain ring A site with none of the above

Tap any site to choose it. A short legend beside the outline repeats the four labels: This dose, Last time, Before that and Suggested.

How the suggested site is worked out

titra looks at your logged doses and finds the site you used least recently, giving preference to a different area from your last dose. A site you have never logged counts as the least recent of all. When you open the dose log, that site is already selected, which is why logging can be a single tap.

The labels say to rotate injection sites with each dose [1][2], and the app repeats that line on the zoomed screen. The suggestion is titra's way of showing which site sits furthest back in your own history. It is worked out from dates, not from anything about your body, and you can choose any other site.

The zoomed grid

Under the outline, a row labelled Pick the exact spot opens one area close up. Over it lies a grid of ten columns, A to J, and ten rows, 1 to 10.

Tap where you injected. titra records the spot, names its cell (B3, say) and sets the nearest named site to match. Then tap Use this spot to go back to the dose log, where the cell shows as a small tag beside the map.

The close-up also shows history for that area:

  • the three most recent spots you marked there, each with its date, the newest drawn darkest
  • a soft green circle on a suggested spot, which is the cell furthest from your recent injections in that area
  • two small cards, one naming the cell you picked and one saying when you last used it, or "Not yet"

Marking a spot is optional. Skip it and the dose is saved with its named site alone.

The keep-clear ring

On the abdomen grid, a dashed oval surrounds the navel. Tap inside it and titra does not record the spot. It shows this instead:

That's too close to the belly button. The instructions say to stay at least 5 cm (2 inches) away.

That distance is the one printed in the instructions for use [1][2]. The oval is an approximation of it on a drawing. It is not measured on your body, so read it as a reminder of what the instructions say and nothing more precise.

Pain score, history and export

Below the map the dose log has an optional pain score from 0 to 10. It is saved with the dose.

Each dose keeps its site. You can see it in the History tab, in the site column of the CSV export and in the PDF summary. The CSV carries the named site; the exact grid spot stays in the app.

What the map does not do

It does not examine anything. titra cannot see your skin, does not know which sites suit you and does not check that a site is suitable. It keeps an accurate note of where you said each injection went, so that next time you are not relying on memory with the pen in your hand.

Quick answers

Is the suggested site medical advice?

No. It is arithmetic on your own log: the site you used least recently, preferring a different area from your last dose. You can pick any site, and the instructions that come with your pen come first.

Do I have to mark an exact spot?

No. The zoomed grid is optional. If you skip it, titra records the named site only, such as left thigh, outer.

Why does the map refuse a tap near the belly button?

On the abdomen grid a dashed oval marks the area around the navel. titra does not record a spot inside it, and shows the line from the instructions about staying at least 5 cm (2 inches) away.

Does the map appear for tablets?

No. The site step is part of the dose log for injected medicines only.
