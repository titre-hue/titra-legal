titra is a GLP-1 dose tracker for iPhone and Android, made by AgentM: it records the doses, weights and side effects you log, and never gives dosing or medical advice. These are the questions we are asked most, answered from how the app behaves today.

Every answer was checked against titra 2.0.2 on 16 November 2026. Where an answer rests on a public page, the number in brackets points to it in the sources at the end.

What is titra?

An app for keeping a clear record of a GLP-1 medicine. You log each dose, and titra shows when the next one is due and where you injected last. Over time it draws your weight, your dose steps and your side effects on one chart.

It runs on iPhone and Android, it is free to download, and it works without an account.

Does titra give medical advice?

No. titra is a personal logging tool. It is not a medical device, it does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition, and it does not give dosing advice.

Some of what it shows comes from outside your own log, and each of those says where it is from: the dose steps on your medicine's label, how often a side effect was reported in the prescribing information, and, if you switch it on, a published trial average. Where the app repeats a line from the label, such as the distance to keep from the navel when injecting, it says the label is speaking.

None of it is a recommendation. Whether and when a dose changes is for your prescriber.

Which medicines does titra support?

GLP-1 medicines taken as a weekly injection, a daily injection or a daily tablet. In the app you choose yours by name, and titra uses that medicine's interval and the dose steps printed on its label.

The list includes Ozempic, Wegovy, the Wegovy pill, Rybelsus, Mounjaro, Saxenda, Victoza and Trulicity, plus an "other" entry each for semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide if yours is not named. You can also type a dose that is not on the list of steps. Not every medicine is sold in every country, so some names will not apply where you live.

Ozempic®, Wegovy®, Rybelsus®, Saxenda® and Victoza® are registered trademarks of Novo Nordisk A/S. Mounjaro® and Trulicity® are registered trademarks of Eli Lilly and Company. titra is not affiliated with or endorsed by either company.

Do I need an account?

No. Logging, reminders, the injection-site map, the timeline and CSV export all work with no account, and nothing is uploaded.

An account exists for one purpose: cloud backup, which is part of titra+. Signing in is how the backup knows whose log it is. You can sign in with Apple on an iPhone, with Google, or with an email address and password. Restoring a titra+ purchase does not need an account.

What leaves my phone, and when?

Your log is stored in a database on your phone. This is everything that can leave it [1]:

What When
Your log and treatment settings Only if you have titra+ and sign in for cloud backup. They are uploaded to Google Firestore.
Crash reports Whenever the app crashes or records an error. They go to Firebase Crashlytics and contain no health entries.
Usage events, such as "dose logged" Only if you switch on Share anonymous usage data. It is off by default, and events never contain doses, weights or symptoms.
Purchase and subscription status When you view or buy titra+, through RevenueCat and the App Store or Google Play.
Your email address Only if you create an account.

Weights imported from Apple Health or Health Connect are treated like weights you typed, so they are uploaded only if cloud backup is on. The longer version is in your data, export and delete, and the full detail is in the privacy policy.

Does titra show ads or track me?

No. There are no ads in titra, no advertising networks and no attribution SDK. The app does not use the advertising identifier, and on Android the permission for it is removed from the app altogether [1].

What is titra+?

An optional subscription. It adds the Insights tab (views built from your own log), weight import from Apple Health or Health Connect, a PDF summary to share with your prescriber, and cloud backup and sync.

Everything you need to keep the log is free: dose logging, reminders, the injection-site map, the timeline, side effects, food noise and CSV export. titra+ is billed by the App Store or Google Play, which show the plans and the price before you pay, and you manage or cancel it in your store account [3][4]. The full split is in what is free and what titra+ adds.

How do reminders work?

You choose one reminder time. titra takes the date of the dose you last logged, adds your medicine's interval (seven days or one day) and reminds you at your time on that day. Log a dose a day late and the next reminder moves a day with it.

If a dose goes unlogged, reminders do not stop. How dose reminders work has the detail, including how far ahead they are planned.

What do reminders show on the lock screen?

By default a reminder reads "Medication reminder" with the line "Your scheduled dose is due. Tap to log it." The name of your medication is left out.

That is a setting called Hide medication name on lock screen, in the You tab under Reminders, and it starts switched on. Turn it off and the reminder names your medication instead.

How do I export my log?

Open the You tab and tap Export CSV. titra builds one file holding every dose, weight, side effect and food-noise rating, and opens your phone's share sheet so you can save it or send it. Export is free and needs no account.

The PDF summary is a different thing: a formatted report of your dose steps, weight and side effects, and part of titra+.

How do I delete my data or my account?

They are two separate actions.

Delete all data, in the You tab, removes every dose, weight, side effect, food-noise rating and setting from the phone after you confirm. It cannot be undone, and it does not touch a cloud backup.

Delete account, under Cloud backup in the You tab, removes the cloud copy of your log and your sign-in account [2]. Your log stays on the phone, and a titra+ subscription keeps running until you cancel it in the App Store or Google Play [3][4]. If you can no longer sign in, the account deletion page explains how to ask by email.

Which languages is titra in?

Twenty-four: English, Arabic, Czech, Danish, Dutch, Finnish, French, German, Hebrew, Hungarian, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Norwegian, Polish, Portuguese (Brazil), Romanian, Slovak, Spanish, Swedish, Thai, Turkish, Ukrainian and Vietnamese.

The app follows your phone's language. To use a different one, open the You tab and tap Language.

Which phones does it run on?

iPhone and iPad, through the App Store, and phones with Android 8.0 or later, through Google Play. titra follows your phone's light or dark setting.

Who makes titra?

AgentM sp. z o.o., an independent company registered in Poland. It is also the data controller named in the privacy policy [1]. No medicine maker is involved in titra, and none endorses it.

The pages on this site are written by the titra team at AgentM. There is more on the about page.

How do I get help?

Write to info@titrapp.com. It helps to say which phone you have and which version of titra, shown at the bottom of the You tab.

The help articles explain how each part of the app behaves. If something on this site does not match your app, tell us and we will correct the page or the app, and log the change.
